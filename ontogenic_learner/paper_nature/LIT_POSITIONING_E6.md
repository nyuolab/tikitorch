# Literature Positioning — E6 Companion Manuscript

**Working title (positioning frame):** *Reopening a closed developmental window in a lifelong embodied predictive-coding agent.*

Web-grounded (2016–2026), venue-verified. Every citation below was checked against arXiv / OpenReview / PMLR / publisher pages during this search. The document is organized as the manuscript's related-work spine: five themes (A–E), each mapped to the five pre-registered results, followed by a per-claim novelty verdict, a must-carry citation list, and three overclaim traps.

## The five results being positioned

1. **Arrival pathology.** A mature always-on agent never ignites a novel sparse-reward world: return exactly 0, acting entropy exactly 0 (n=8 lives, 2 scales, 2 LR schedules). Cause = **action-head logit saturation** — top1–top2 margins of 19–37 nats, greedy argmax bitwise-frozen from ~2.2M steps.
2. **Surprise-load entropy restoration.** A 3-arm checkpoint-branch falsifier shows cross-task acting-entropy restoration is driven by **surprising input forcing trunk adaptation**: a **scrambled-observation (unlearnable) stream restores entropy as well as a genuine novel world**, while extra familiar residency does not.
3. **The cure.** A **forward-KL-to-uniform** regularizer on the action readout — the gradient of λ·CE(U, π) = λ·D_KL(U‖π) up to a constant, **not** the reverse-KL max-ent term −λ·H(π) — applied **at training time** ignites novel-world learning (5/5 + 3/3 + 3/3). An **acting-time ε-exploration floor fails 0/5** (a learner-vs-actor dissociation). Dose-calibrated at λ = 2⁻⁴.
4. **Age-penalty abolished.** Under matched treatment, treated adults acquire the novel world **at parity with treated newborns**; the pull also helps newborns (+61%). We corrected our own earlier "adult beats newborn" claim by adding the matched control.
5. **Interference.** World-model **storage displacement with preserved features**: 50k serve-only (frozen-weight, inference-only) steps recover nothing; gradient relearning recovers ~20 nats in 1–3k steps (~**100× faster** than initial acquisition). EWC-anchor and IDBD (θ=0.01) **fail** the pre-registered consolidation bars.

Theme→claim map: A→(3); B→(1,4); C→(1,4); D→(5); E→(2).

## Theme A — Entropy regularization and the KL-direction distinction (result 3)

### What is established

**The uniform-target regularizer and its two directions are 30+ years old and fully characterized.**

- **Szegedy, Vanhoucke, Ioffe, Shlens & Wojna (2016), CVPR** — "Rethinking the Inception Architecture for Computer Vision." Label smoothing mixes the one-hot target with uniform u; the added term is exactly **forward KL, D_KL(u‖p)** ("uniform relative to model"), up to the constant H(u). This is the *same direction* as our λ·CE(U, π). (arXiv:1512.00567)
- **Pereyra, Tucker, Chorowski, Kaiser & Hinton (2017), ICLR Workshop** — "Regularizing Neural Networks by Penalizing Confident Output Distributions." *The* citation for the direction split: the confidence penalty is −β·H(p) = D_KL(p‖u) − log K = **reverse KL to uniform**, and the paper explicitly "connect[s] a maximum entropy based confidence penalty to label smoothing through the direction of the KL divergence." (arXiv:1701.06548)
- **Meister, Salesky & Cotterell (2020), ACL** — "Generalized Entropy Regularization or: There's Nothing Special about Label Smoothing." Proves the forward-KL (label-smoothing) member **assigns infinite cost to zero-probability outputs** — i.e., forward-KL-to-uniform keeps a *non-vanishing gradient on collapsed mass*. In NMT this is framed as a drawback; **we invert it into the mechanism** (the pull's gradient grows exactly where an entropy bonus's gradient vanishes). (ACL 2020.acl-main.615)
- **Max-ent RL = reverse KL to uniform.** Ziebart, Maas, Bagnell & Dey (2008, AAAI) and Ziebart (2010, CMU thesis); Haarnoja, Tang, Abbeel & Levine (2017, ICML, soft Q-learning); **Haarnoja, Zhou, Abbeel & Levine (2018), ICML** (SAC). All maximize H(π) = −D_KL(π‖U) + const — the *reverse* direction, the one whose gradient dies when logits saturate.
- **KL direction is behaviorally load-bearing in RL.** Chan, Silva, Lim, Kozuno, Mahmood & White (2022), JMLR 23(253), "Greedification Operators…: Forward and Reverse KL": reverse KL has stronger improvement guarantees; **forward KL induces more exploration**. GX-Chen, Prakash, Guo, Fergus & Ranganath (2025), "KL-Regularized RL is Designed to Mode Collapse" (arXiv:2510.20817), cautions that at low regularization strength direction alone does not guarantee diversity — cite as the counterpoint.

**The closest theoretical precedent to result 3.** **Agarwal, Kakade, Lee & Mahajan (2021), JMLR 22(98)** — "On the Theory of Policy Gradient Methods." Their **log-barrier regularizer is literally λ·KL(Unif_A ‖ π_θ)** — forward KL to uniform over actions — chosen *instead of* the entropy bonus precisely because entropy is too weak to prevent vanishing gradients as softmax policies go near-deterministic; with it they prove softmax PG converges to near-optimal stationary points. This is our exact object deployed as a *convergence/trainability* device. Supporting: Ahmed, Le Roux, Norouzi & Schuurmans (2019, ICML, "Understanding the Impact of Entropy on Policy Optimization") — entropy's benefit is substantially **optimization-landscape smoothing, not just exploration** — the nearest conceptual foreshadow of a learner-side (not actor-side) mechanism.

**Entropy-collapse fixes in RLVR / LLM-RL (2024–2026)** — a crowded, fast-moving field, but the specific lever is absent. DAPO (Yu et al., 2025, arXiv:2503.14476; clip-higher). Cui et al. (2025), "The Entropy Mechanism of RL for Reasoning Language Models" (arXiv:2505.22617) — entropy collapse governed by covariance(log-prob, logit-update); fixes **Clip-Cov / KL-Cov** (KL penalty on high-covariance tokens) — the *mechanistically closest cousin* of our head pull, but framed as exploration loss in stationary RLVR, not plasticity, and **KL-to-reference, not KL-to-uniform**. AEnt (2025, arXiv:2509.03493) explicitly argues *against* uniform-pulling in large action spaces (bias O(H·log(|A|/|A*|^{1/H}))) — important scope caveat. AEPO/SCOPE-RL (2025, arXiv:2510.08141): decoding-time temperature does **not** prevent entropy collapse; only a training-time term does — the nearest published analogue of our training-vs-acting dissociation, in a different setting. Forward-vs-reverse KL is also live in alignment: Wang et al. (2024, ICLR, "Beyond Reverse KL"); Go et al. (2023, ICML, f-divergence alignment) — but all regularize toward a **reference model**, never uniform.

**Nearest deployed relative (honesty cite).** Hafner et al., **DreamerV3** ("Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models," arXiv:2301.04104, 2023; *Nature* 640:647–653, 2025) uses **"unimix": actor categoricals = 99% network + 1% uniform** "to keep log probabilities and KL divergences well behaved." That is a fixed, always-on, parametrization-level uniform floor motivated by numerical stability — **not** an isolated, dosed, training-time forward-KL regularizer, and never evaluated as an ignition rescue.

**Learner-vs-actor dissociation, supporting anchors.** Schaul, Barreto, Quan & Ostrovski (2022, NeurIPS, "The Phenomenon of Policy Churn") — the greedy policy changes on ~10% of states per handful of updates as *implicit exploration from training updates*, and "ε-noise plays a much smaller role than expected." Our bitwise-frozen argmax is the **degenerate churn→0 limit**, which kills exactly the mechanism that carries exploration. NoisyNets (Fortunato et al., 2018, ICLR) and Parameter Space Noise (Plappert et al., 2018, ICLR) show *where* stochasticity is injected matters (weight-space beats action-space), but both still act through behavior.

### Where our result sits + honest delta

The regularizer is inherited (Szegedy 2016; Pereyra 2017; Agarwal 2021; unimix). The direction distinction is established (Pereyra 2017; Meister 2020; Chan 2022). "Entropy helps the learner, not just the actor" is foreshadowed (Ahmed 2019; Schaul 2022). **What we could not locate anywhere in 2016–2026:** (i) forward-KL-to-uniform isolated *empirically* as a **plasticity/acquisition rescue** that re-ignites learning of a novel sparse-reward world in a saturated mature agent, and (ii) a **controlled learner-vs-actor dissociation** where a training-time uniform pull succeeds (5/5) while a matched acting-time ε-floor fails (0/5). **Thin margins to declare:** the mechanism margin is genuinely thin (position result 3 as "the log-barrier of Agarwal et al., transplanted from convergence theory into a lifelong agent and shown to be *causally sufficient* for ignition"); the two experimental claims appear clean, with caveats that (a) RLVR's training-time-only fixes are spiritually adjacent, (b) AEnt argues against uniform-pulling in *large* action spaces — scope the claim to small/moderate action spaces, and (c) DreamerV3's unimix and AEPO's temperature-fails result are the two thinnest margins and must be cited.

## Theme B — Loss-of-plasticity cures and the parity question (results 1, 4)

### What is established

The loss-of-plasticity canon (venues verified):

- **Dohare, Hernandez-Garcia, Lan, Rahman, Mahmood & Sutton (2024), *Nature* 632:768–774** — "Loss of plasticity in deep continual learning." Backprop decays to linear-net level by ~task 2000 (Continual ImageNet); **continual backprop** (reinit low-utility units) maintains plasticity; also documents PPO policy collapse on Slippery Ant. **This is prevention-from-birth, not rescue of a matured net.**
- **Sokar, Agarwal, Castro & Evci (2023), ICML** — "The Dormant Neuron Phenomenon in Deep RL" (ReDo). Dormant units accumulate; recycling avoids collapse at high replay ratios. Recovery reported as "very close to" the *healthy low-replay baseline*, not a fresh network. (arXiv:2302.12902)
- **Nikishin, Oh, Ostrovski, Lyle, Pascanu, Dabney & Barreto (2023), NeurIPS** — "Plasticity Injection." Adds fresh head parameters (h_θ + h′₁ − h′₂), applied **to the head**, preserving predictions. Recovery is **explicitly partial/heterogeneous**: ~20% aggregate over resets/S&P; per-game 12 consistent / 18 minor / 24 negligible / 3 negative. No fresh-parity claim.
- **Nikishin, Schwarzer, D'Oro, Bacon & Courville (2022), ICML** — "The Primacy Bias in Deep RL." Periodic resets (last layer for SPR; whole nets for SAC) recover strongly (SAC RR=32: >+100%), **but the replay buffer is the knowledge-transfer mechanism and the reset net is literally a newborn** — parity by construction, and unavailable in our no-replay setting. Its §3.1–3.2 **heavy-priming experiment is our closest adult-vs-newborn analogue**: an over-primed SAC net cannot recover even after ~1M fresh transitions while a fresh net given the same buffer learns rapidly — but their remedy is *replacement*, not *treatment*.
- **Juliani & Ash (2024), NeurIPS (spotlight)** — "A Study of Plasticity Loss in On-Policy Deep RL." The cleanest fresh-referenced RL design. Regenerative methods (soft S&P, L2-init, ReDo, LayerNorm) "consistently mitigate" but **final rewards remain below the reset-all (fresh-per-task) baseline**; plasticity injection *underperforms warm-start*; **final-layer reset fails in all three shift conditions** (a useful contrast: our cure acts at the head but *treats* rather than *resets* it). (arXiv:2405.19153)
- **Ash & Adams (2020), NeurIPS** — "On Warm-Starting Neural Network Training." The strongest *parity* claim in the literature — but **supervised and stationary**: shrink-and-perturb closes the warm-start generalization gap (~4.5pp on CIFAR-10) by *partially re-randomizing weights* (θ = λθ + γθ_init), i.e., parity by making the network partly newborn.
- Supporting: Lyle et al. (2022, ICLR, capacity loss / InFeR); Lyle et al. (2023, ICML, "Understanding Plasticity" — plasticity loss **"occurs in the absence of saturated units,"** LayerNorm most effective — the field has *not* converged on a locus, and actively de-emphasizes saturation); Lyle et al. (2024, "Disentangling…," separates input- vs target-non-stationarity); Kumar, Marklund & Van Roy (2023/CoLLAs 2025, L2-Init); Elsayed & Mahmood (2024, ICLR, UPGD); Elsayed, Lan, Lyle & Mahmood (2024, RLC, weight clipping — mitigates "policy collapse," reduces tanh-unit saturation); Abbas et al. (2023, CoLLAs — CReLU "match[es] or exceed[s]" the reset agent, but architecture-from-birth); Ma et al. (2024, ICLR, "Revisiting Plasticity in Visual RL" — the **critic** is the bottleneck *module*; the opposite locus from ours); Klein et al. (2024–26 survey, arXiv:2411.04832 — defines plasticity loss as the gap to a fresh network yet **never claims any method reaches parity**).

**Mechanism parallel for result 1.** The action-head logit-saturation locus with quantified margins (19–37 nats) and bitwise-frozen argmax is *not documented* as the plasticity locus. Nearest: policy-collapse framing (Dohare, Lan & Mahmood, "Overcoming Policy Collapse in Deep RL," EWRL 2023 — **read before submission; it is your nearest neighbor**); tanh-unit saturation (Elsayed 2024); neural collapse of the last layer (Papyan, Han & Donoho, 2020, PNAS 117:24652 — the supervised twin); and the RLVR logit-covariance entropy-collapse account (Cui et al. 2025). Schaul et al.'s ~10%-per-update churn gives a published "healthy" reference against which frozen argmax is a striking anomaly.

### Where our result sits + honest delta

**Parity (result 4) appears to be an unclaimed cell.** Prevention methods (CBP, L2-init, weight clipping) never rescue a matured net; reset methods reach parity only by *installing newborn parameters*; post-hoc treatments (ReDo, injection) report explicitly partial/heterogeneous recovery; the only clean parity claims (Ash & Adams; Elsayed's CIFAR-10) are **supervised warm-start**. **Matched-treatment adult = newborn parity on acquiring a novel sparse-reward world, in an always-on no-replay embodied agent, with the adult's prior competence retained** (cure without destroying the old policy) is not shown. **Result 1's saturated-head locus** with quantified margins is likewise undocumented; note the honesty tension that Lyle 2023 finds plasticity loss *without* saturated units — so frame ours as *one* saturation-driven pathway in *this* substrate, not a universal mechanism. **Thin margins:** (a) several parity-relevant comparisons live only in figures (ReDo; Juliani & Ash Fig. 4); (b) Dohare's CIFAR-100 "eliminated altogether" and Elsayed's "removes the generalization gap" can be adversarially read as parity; (c) the EWRL policy-collapse paper is unread in full.

## Theme C — Critical periods and *reopening* a closed window (results 1, 4)

### What is established

- **Achille, Rovere & Soatto (2019), ICLR** — "Critical Learning Periods in Deep Neural Networks" (arXiv:1711.08856, first posted 2017). The foundational reference and **the sharpest contrast for our reopening claim**. An early blur deficit causes irreversible loss of high-frequency filters; the driver is a Fisher-Information-trace rise-then-fall = "**loss of Information Plasticity**." Verbatim: *"the effects of sensory deficits during a critical period cannot be overcome, no matter how much additional training is performed."* Their whole thesis is **closure irreversibility**; they never attempt reopening.
- **Kleinman, Achille & Soatto (2024), ICLR (Spotlight)** — "Critical Learning Periods Emerge Even in Deep Linear Networks" (arXiv:2308.12221). Critical periods appear even in deep *linear* nets (source/pathway competition, amplified by depth) — "may be fundamental to learning and not an accident of biology." Permanence framing; no reopening. (Related: Kleinman et al. 2023, CVPR, multisensory-integration critical periods.)
- **Golatkar, Achille & Soatto (2019), NeurIPS** — "Time Matters in Regularizing Deep Networks" (**correction: NeurIPS 2019, not CVPR**; arXiv:1905.13277). Regularization applied only *after* the early transient "has no effect on the final solution" — *when* you regularize is decisive. Supports our always-on-regularizer framing.
- **Critical periods in RL.** Park et al. (2022), "On the Importance of Critical Period in Multi-stage RL" (arXiv:2208.04832) — **ICML 2022 *Workshop*, not main track / not AAAI; verify before citing.** de Kleijn, Sen & Kachergis (2022), *Topics in Cognitive Science* — a robot-arm agent that gets a new objective too late cannot flexibly incorporate it (the closest embodied "closed window for a new goal" analogue). Park et al. (2024, AAAI), "Toddler-Inspired Reward Transition." **All study window formation/exploitation; none reopens a closed window.**

**Reopening — the crux (two adjacent literatures, declared honestly).**

- **Direct challenge to permanence (supervised):** Pawlak (2025), "On the Occurrence of Critical Learning Periods in Neural Networks" (arXiv:2510.09687) — a **learning-rate reset / cyclic LR** after the deficit makes "the performance gap disappear entirely." The single closest reopening/recovery claim, but the lever is a *global LR reset* in *supervised* classification, not a targeted head-level intervention in RL.
- **Loss-of-plasticity restoration (Theme B):** resets (Nikishin 2022), ReDo (Sokar 2023), injection (Nikishin 2023), continual backprop (Dohare 2024) all restore *general trainability* — but by **parameter surgery** (reset / recycle / add capacity), not a loss term, and none frames it as reopening a *characterized developmental window* or reports adult-newborn parity.
- **Neuroscience reopening (the clean framing bridge):** Hensch (2005), *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 6:877 (windows gated by PV+/GABAergic maturation + ECM); **Pizzorusso et al. (2002), *Science* 298:1248** (chondroitinase-ABC degradation of perineuronal nets **reopens** the closed ocular-dominance window in adult rats); Maya-Vetencourt et al. (2008), *Science* 320:385 (fluoxetine reopens adult OD plasticity via lowered inhibition + BDNF); Bavelier, Levi, Li, Dan & Hensch (2010), *J. Neurosci.* 30:14964 ("Removing Brakes on Adult Brain Plasticity"). **No ML paper invokes reopening ocular-dominance plasticity as the analogy for a reopening *method*** — that framing is open for us to claim. Our forward-KL-to-uniform pull, which restores E/I-like stochasticity and undoes logit saturation, is the computational analogue of *lowering the inhibitory brake*.

### Where our result sits + honest delta

The dedicated critical-period lineage (Achille 2019; Kleinman 2024; Golatkar 2019) treats closure as **permanent and never reopens it**, and no RL critical-period paper (Park 2022; de Kleijn 2022) demonstrates recovery after closure — so an **active reopening** result is genuinely novel against that literature. **Thin margins:** "restoring plasticity" per se is not unprecedented — Pawlak 2025 disputes critical-period permanence via a global LR reset, and the loss-of-plasticity line restores trainability via surgery. Our defensible novelty is the **conjunction**: a *hard, saturated closure* (bitwise-frozen argmax, return/entropy exactly 0, n=8) reopened by a **targeted, information-preserving forward-KL-to-uniform pull on the action head** — no weight reset, no added capacity, no global LR change — returning treated adults to *newborn parity* on novel sparse-reward worlds. Position it explicitly as reopening a *specific characterized critical window in RL*, distinguished from reset-/injection-based recovery and framed with the Pizzorusso/Maya-Vetencourt/Hensch "reopen the window" analogy.

## Theme D — Continual-RL interference: displacement vs shadowing, and savings (result 5)

### What is established

- **Foundations.** McCloskey & Cohen (1989), *Psych. Learn. Motiv.* 24 (catastrophic interference); French (1999), *TiCS* 3(4) (overlapping distributed representations; already frames the **savings/relearning** measure); Kirkpatrick et al. (2017), *PNAS* 114:3521 (**EWC** — diagonal-Fisher anchor, demonstrated on sequential Atari).
- **Erasure vs retrieval-failure (the shadowing/displacement axis).** Davari, Asadi, Mudur, Aljundi & Belilovsky (2022), CVPR — "Probing Representation Forgetting": linear-probe-measured forgetting is far smaller than accuracy collapse, i.e., much "forgetting" is readout-level (**shadowing**) — but the probe itself is *gradient-trained*. Ramasesh, Dyer & Raghu (2021), ICLR — deeper layers **erase** earlier-task subspaces (a displacement finding at the layer level); forgetting maximal at intermediate task similarity. Anthes et al. (2023, CCN); Trivedi & Melwani (2026, arXiv:2606.06032, classifier-reset recovers 75.7%); Filus et al. (2026, arXiv:2605.16374) — all supervised, all recover via *gradient* steps. **Genuinely gradient-free recovery** exists only in LLM unlearning: Zhang et al. (2025, ICLR) — "Catastrophic Failure of LLM Unlearning via Quantization" (83% of "forgotten" knowledge resurfaces after 4-bit quantization, zero training) proves suppression-not-erasure is *detectable without gradients*.
- **Savings / fast relearning.** Hetherington & Seidenberg (1989), *Proc. CogSci* 11 (introduced the savings measure into the interference debate); Davidson & Mozer (2020), CVPR — "Sequential Mastery…": relearning epochs-to-criterion fall roughly **power-law** with exposures (multi-fold, **no ~100× figure**); unlearning "relearning attacks" (Hu et al., arXiv:2406.13356 — 5 samples restore "unlearned" knowledge). Ash & Adams (2020), NeurIPS — the caveat that fast relearning ≠ recovered generalization.
- **Continual-RL interference.** Khetarpal, Riemer, Rish & Precup (2022), JAIR 75 (the review/taxonomy); Wołczyk et al. (2021, NeurIPS, "Continual World" — forgetting-tuned methods incl. EWC get **poor/negative forward transfer**); Wołczyk et al. (2022, NeurIPS, "Disentangling Transfer" — actor/critic/exploration decomposition); Kessler et al. (2023, CoLLAs, world models for continual RL). The closest single precedent is a **2026 unreviewed single-author preprint** (Nijjer, arXiv:2607.19749, DreamerV3 component probes) whose verdict is the **mirror image** of ours (their WM *shadows*, ours *displaces*) and whose recovery tests are still gradient-based.
- **EWC & IDBD specifics.** EWC fails/hurts in RL (Continual World; MEAL, arXiv:2506.14990; Schwarz et al. 2018 "Progress & Compress" online-EWC remedy; Huszár's PNAS-comment critique). IDBD = **Sutton (1992), AAAI** ("Adapting Bias by Gradient Descent"). Meta-learned representations for continual learning = **Javed & White (2019), NeurIPS** (**correction: Javed & White, not "Javed, Mahmood, Sutton"**).

### Where our result sits + honest delta

The concepts are established separately — suppressed-vs-erased (Davari; Ramasesh; Zhang 2025), savings (Hetherington & Seidenberg 1989; Davidson & Mozer 2020), component-level continual-RL analysis (Wołczyk; Nijjer). **What is unprecedented is the instrument + composition:** a **zero-gradient serve-only exposure phase** that operationally separates shadowing from displacement in a lifelong RL world model, returning a clean **displacement** verdict (0 recovery in 50k serve-only steps) *alongside* preserved-feature probes *and* a quantified **~100× relearning speedup** (the sharpest savings figure on record; claim "we quantify savings at ~100×," not "savings is new"), *plus* **first pre-registered consolidation-bar failures for EWC-anchor and IDBD(θ=0.01)** in this setting. **Thin margins:** supervised-CL probing already says "forgetting is mostly accessibility, knowledge persists," and three 2026 preprints converge on erasure-vs-suppression taxonomies — so claim the discriminator and the RL-world-model *displacement* finding, never "first to ask whether forgetting is erasure." Pre-empt the reviewer objection that serve-only recovery is vacuous for stateless nets: the test is informative because the recurrent world model carries runtime state that could in principle re-index shadowed knowledge (the LLM quantization result proves gradient-free resurfacing is possible when knowledge is merely masked).

## Theme E — Cross-task exploration restoration, incl. unlearnable input (result 2)

### What is established

- **Curiosity / noisy-TV.** Pathak et al. (2017, ICML, ICM); **Burda, Edwards, Storkey & Klimov (2019), ICLR** (RND — explicitly engineered *against* the **noisy-TV problem**: prediction-error curiosity is attracted to unpredictable/scrambled input); Burda et al. (2018/19, "Large-Scale Study of Curiosity" — random features suffice; directly demonstrates the noisy-TV stall). **Sign-inverted precedent:** noisy-TV treats attraction to unlearnable input as a *pathology to remove*; we assign the same primitive the **opposite role** — scrambled input is a benign distribution-shift forcing function on the trunk, and restored entropy is a downstream consequence, not a reward the agent chases.
- **Input-shift drives representation change / transient reactivation.** **Sokar et al. (2023), ICML** — dormant units are frozen "unless the unit's input distribution shifts," and a sudden environment change **transiently raises the activated-neuron rate** — the closest mechanistic analogue to our finding. Lyle et al. (2024, "Disentangling…") **separates input- from target-non-stationarity** — the exact axis our result lives on. Lyle et al. (2022, ICLR, capacity loss); Igl et al. (2021, ICLR, transient non-stationarity imprints the latent representation); Schaul et al. (2022, policy churn as training-update-driven implicit exploration).
- **Unlearnable input still drives trunk adaptation.** Zhang, Bengio, Hardt, Recht & Vinyals (2017, ICLR — nets fit pure random labels); **Maennel et al. (2020), NeurIPS** — "What Do Neural Networks Learn When Trained With Random Labels?" (random labels still induce *structured* representation change — the strongest "unlearnable → real trunk adaptation" precedent); Baradad et al. (2021, NeurIPS — representations from procedural noise transfer to real vision). Reward-side analogue: Spurious Rewards / RLVR (2025, arXiv:2506.10947).

### Where our result sits + honest delta

The ingredients are each anticipated: noisy-TV (unlearnable input uniquely engages a network), the plasticity line (input shift reactivates representations independent of reward), and the random-label line (unlearnable input drives real trunk change). **No single claim is unprecedented, and the mechanism margin ("surprising input → trunk adaptation") is genuinely thin.** The likely-novel contribution is the **controlled falsifier and the composition**: a directly-measured causal demonstration that acting-policy entropy, having collapsed to *exactly* 0, is restored by **input surprise per se** — with a **scrambled/unlearnable stream performing on par with a genuine novel world** and mere familiar residency failing — cleanly dissociating *surprise/distribution-shift* from *task learnability* as the exploration-restoring variable. That dissociation was not found anywhere. **This is the most unusual of the five claims.** Cite Sokar 2023 and Lyle 2024 as the mechanism precedent and RND/Large-Scale-Curiosity as the sign-inverted noisy-TV precedent; concede up front that "unlearnable input changes representations" and "input shift reactivates networks" are established.

## Three overclaim traps

1. **"We introduce a new regularizer / the first uniform-target term on the action head."** False as stated. Forward-KL-to-uniform *is* label smoothing (Szegedy 2016); an always-on uniform floor on the action readout *is* DreamerV3's unimix (Hafner); λ·KL(Unif‖π) over actions *is* Agarwal et al.'s (2021) log-barrier. Our novelty is **isolating the KL direction as the causal plasticity-rescue lever and the training-vs-acting dissociation — not the regularizer.** Additional guard: AEnt (2509.03493) argues uniform-pulling is *harmful* in large action spaces — **scope the claim to small/moderate action spaces.**

2. **"We reopen a closed plasticity window / provide the first plasticity cure."** A whole loss-of-plasticity cure literature exists (Dohare 2024; Nikishin resets 2022 & injection 2023; Sokar ReDo 2023; L2-init), Pawlak (2025) already disputes critical-period *permanence* via a global LR reset, and biology reopens windows routinely (Pizzorusso 2002). Claim only the **specific conjunction**: restoration to **newborn-parity under matched treatment** in a mature, no-replay, history-retaining embodied agent, achieved by a **loss term rather than parameter surgery, reset, or added capacity** — an unclaimed cell — and cite the reopening as an *analogy* to (not a re-derivation of) removing molecular brakes. (Also avoid the memory-flagged trap of equating cascade PC with a novel continual-learning rule — that is Z-IL, not new.)

3. **"We discover that forgetting is displacement-not-shadowing / discover savings / show cross-task entropy restoration as a new phenomenon."** Suppression-vs-erasure is established (Davari 2022; Ramasesh 2021; Zhang 2025 ICLR), savings dates to Hetherington & Seidenberg (1989), and the surprise-load ingredients are anticipated by noisy-TV (Burda 2019) and random-label representation change (Maennel 2020). Claim the **instruments and controlled dissociations** ("we *quantify* savings at ~100×"; "a zero-gradient serve-only discriminator returns a *displacement* verdict"; "unlearnable input restores entropy *on par with* a real novel world"), never the underlying phenomena.
