# Cascade = Relaxation in Piecewise-Linear Predictive Coding

*Manuscript theory section. The proof below is exact for piecewise-linear networks; §5 (the
tropical-dominated transformer) is the open extension the depth-resolved experiments resolve.*

---

## 1. Predictive coding: inference and learning

Consider a feedforward network of `L` layers with weights `{W_l}`. Predictive coding (PC) augments each
layer with **value nodes** `x_l` and treats the feedforward maps as *predictions*
`μ_l = f_l(x_{l−1}; W_l)`. The prediction errors are `ε_l = x_l − μ_l`, and the variational free energy
(Gaussian, unit precision) is

  **F({x_l}; {W_l}) = ½ Σ_l ‖ x_l − f_l(x_{l−1}; W_l) ‖².**

Training alternates two phases:
- **Inference** — with weights fixed and the boundary nodes clamped (`x_0` = input; supervised: `x_L` =
  target), relax the hidden value nodes to a stationary point of `F` under the gradient flow
  `ẋ_l = −∂F/∂x_l`.
- **Learning** — at the inference stationary point, take a step `−η ∂F/∂W_l`.

The inference gradient is

  **∂F/∂x_l = ε_l − J_{l+1}(x_l)ᵀ ε_{l+1},   J_{l+1}(x_l) := ∂f_{l+1}/∂x_l.**   (1)

Whittington & Bogacz (2017): at the inference equilibrium (`∂F/∂x_l = 0` for all hidden `l`) the error nodes
`ε_l` coincide with the backpropagation error signals and `∂F/∂W_l` equals the backprop weight gradient.
Reaching that equilibrium in general requires **iterating** (1) to convergence.

## 2. The two backward schemes

**Relaxation (iterative).** Integrate `ẋ_l = −∂F/∂x_l` for `t = 1…T` until `‖∂F/∂x‖ < tol`; read off
`{ε_l}`; update `W`. Cost: `T` inference sweeps.

**Cascade (single pass).** Set `ε_L` from the clamped output, propagate once in reverse
`ε_l ← J_{l+1}ᵀ ε_{l+1}`, update `W`. Cost: one sweep. For exact Jacobians the cascade errors are the
backprop errors *by construction*; the question is whether they coincide with the relaxation equilibrium.

## 3. Why smooth networks must iterate

If each `f_l` is smooth and nonlinear, `J_{l+1}(x_l)` **depends on `x_l`**. The equilibrium condition
`ε_l = J_{l+1}(x_l)ᵀ ε_{l+1}` is then a *nonlinear* fixed-point problem: as inference moves `x_l`, the
prediction `μ` and hence `J` change — the linearization about which the errors are computed "moves under
you." A single sweep uses the Jacobians at the forward-pass point; the true equilibrium uses the Jacobians
at the *settled* point. Iteration is the mechanism that reconciles them, and its convergence and stability
degrade with depth — **this is the crux of the open problem of training deep PC.**

## 4. Piecewise-linear networks: the equilibrium is reached in one exact pass

Now suppose each `f_l` is **continuous piecewise-linear (PWL)** — the class generated by affine maps, `max`/
`min`, `ReLU`, and `argmax`-gather (selection). This includes tropical / max-plus layers
(`(f(x))_i = max_j (W_{ij} + x_j)`), `ReLU`, max-pool, hard (argmax-gather) attention, and ordinary — hence
also **dyadically-quantized** — linear maps.

**Lemma 1 (constant Jacobian per region).** A continuous PWL map `f` partitions its domain into closed
polyhedral regions on whose interiors `f` is affine. On each region the Jacobian `J = ∂f/∂x` is a
**constant** matrix, independent of `x`. For the tropical / ReLU / gather primitives this constant is a 0/1
**selection** matrix (each output row selects its argmax / active input); for a linear (or dyadic-linear)
layer it is the constant weight matrix itself.

**Theorem 1 (cascade = relaxation).** Let the network be feedforward and PWL. Fix the input; let `R` be the
polyhedral region selected by the forward pass (the argmax / active-unit choices realized at the forward
activations). *If the inference trajectory remains in `R`* (no selection flips during settling), then the
one-pass **cascade** error equals the converged **relaxation** equilibrium **exactly**, and both equal the
backpropagation gradient of the affine restriction `f|_R`.

*Proof.* On `R` every `J_l` is the constant matrix of Lemma 1, so `F` restricted to `R` is a convex
quadratic in `{x_l}` with constant Hessian, and the stationarity condition (1) = 0 becomes the **linear**
system `ε_l = J_{l+1}ᵀ ε_{l+1}` (hidden `l`), with `ε_L` fixed by the output clamp. Because the network is
feedforward, the dependency graph is acyclic, so this linear system is **triangular**: it is solved exactly
by a single backward substitution in reverse topological order — which is *precisely the cascade sweep*.
Hence the cascade errors already satisfy the stationarity condition; being the stationary point of a convex
quadratic, they are its unique minimizer, i.e. the relaxation equilibrium. The relaxation flow
`ẋ = −∂F/∂x` is the gradient flow of this convex quadratic and converges (in exact arithmetic) to that same
unique minimizer from any initialization in `R`. Therefore **cascade = converged relaxation.** ∎

**Corollary 1 (the break condition).** The equivalence can fail *only* if the inference trajectory leaves
`R` — i.e., some argmax / active-unit selection flips during settling. Synchronous inference that holds
selections at their forward-pass values never leaves `R`, giving exact (bit-for-bit) equivalence.
Asynchronous or large-step inference may cross a region boundary, incurring a small divergence.

> This matches our measurements: **synchronous relaxation is bit-exact to cascade; asynchronous relaxation
> differs by ≈0.6 pp** — the divergence attributable to boundary-crossing selection flips.

**Remark (why deep PC becomes tractable here).** Theorem 1 says that in the PWL setting the iterative
inference that makes smooth deep PC unstable **collapses to a single exact backward sweep**. There is
nothing to iterate toward within a region, so the depth-related instability of PC inference does not arise.
Equivalently, the cheap cascade computes the *true* PC equilibrium, not an approximation of it — which is
exactly what a fixed, feedforward, no-global-sync hardware pass does. The property that makes deep PC hard
(iterative settling) is *absent by construction* in a piecewise-linear substrate.

## 5. The tropical-dominated transformer (open — resolved by experiment)

Our distributed transformer is PWL **except** at two ops: the softmax of the warm-trained attention and
LayerNorm — both smooth. Theorem 1 therefore applies exactly to the dyadic-linear layers, ReLU, and the
(deploy-time) argmax-gather attention, but not a priori at softmax / LN. Two questions remain, which the
depth-resolved experiments settle:

  (i) Does the smooth fraction inject enough Jacobian variation to break cascade ≈ relaxation, and does the
  break **compound with depth**?

  (ii) If the network is *tropical-dominated* — the smooth ops a small, residual-bypassed fraction — does
  the cascade remain an equilibrium of the full relaxation to within a controlled tolerance at **all**
  depths?

**Prediction.** Under the residual stream, the smooth ops (softmax, LN) are a bounded fraction of each
block's Jacobian and are bypassed by the identity path; we expected the cascade–relaxation divergence to be
bounded in depth rather than compounding.

**MEASURED (Experiment 1, 2026-07-05).** The prediction is *exceeded*: cascade and synchronous relaxation
are **BIT-EXACT on the real tropical-dominated transformer** — softmax attention and LayerNorm present — at
every depth measured. On D=256, H=4, FFN=1024, SEQ=128, VOCAB=256 (byte-level enwik8), trained to
convergence:

| depth L | grad cos(cascade, relax_sync) | per-block eps divergence | selection flips |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | **1.000000** | [0, 0] | 0 |
| 4 | **1.000000** | [0, 0, 0, 0] | 0 |
| 8 | **1.000000** | [0×8] | 0 |
| 16 | **0.9999999999999998** (bit-exact within float eps) | [~2e-16] | 0 |

The synchronous relaxation converges in **one iteration** (Theorem 1) and matches cascade to the last bit,
with **zero selection flips** — even though softmax and LN are smooth. Interpretation: the residual stream
makes each block a small perturbation of the identity, so the block Jacobian is dominated by the PWL
(dyadic-linear + ReLU + gather) path; the smooth softmax/LN contribute no measurable Jacobian variation
across the (clamped, single-pass) inference. **The tropical-dominated transformer inherits Theorem 1
exactly** — cascade IS the relaxation equilibrium, so the cheap no-global-sync hardware pass computes the
true PC solution, not an approximation, at every depth. (Asynchronous relaxation diverges as Corollary 1
predicts — grad cos 0.65 @L=2, 0.82 @L=4 — the selection-order effect, and it does *not* worsen with depth.)

This is the mechanism behind deep-PC tractability in this substrate: the iterative settling that destabilizes
smooth deep PC is absent, empirically, on a real transformer.

## 6. Deep-PC traction: the PC–backprop gap across the tropical spectrum (Experiment 2 — PRELIMINARY)

§5 establishes the *mechanism* (cascade = relaxation on the real transformer). Experiment 2 asks whether that
mechanism *buys* anything: does PC's trained accuracy track backprop's, and how does the gap scale with depth,
as the network is moved along the spectrum from purely piecewise-linear to fully smooth? The claim is that
**the more piecewise-linear the network, the flatter the PC–backprop gap with depth**, because PWL structure
lets PC reach its equilibrium — which *equals* backprop (Whittington–Bogacz) — in one exact pass, rather than
by an iterative relaxation that at depth either truncates (finite inference budget) or diverges (ill-conditioned
settling). §5's mechanism is *why* the leftmost row of the table below is flat.

| network | how PC obtains its gradient | PC–backprop gap vs depth | mechanism / why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **pure-PWL tropical** (`SMOOTH_OFF`; dyadic-linear + ReLU + argmax-gather) | cascade = one exact backward pass | **≈ 0, flat to L=16** | Thm 1 (§4): cascade = relaxation = equilibrium = backprop; nothing to iterate |
| **tropical-dominated** (adds softmax attention + LayerNorm) | cascade with frozen selections | **bounded (~0.5 nat), grows slowly** | the two smooth ops inject a bounded linearization error, residual-bypassed (§5) |
| **smooth** (float net, well-conditioned so the relaxation converges) | finite-T iterative relaxation | **grows with depth (truncation); T→convergence → backprop** | T inference sweeps propagate the loss error only T layers; deep layers stay undertrained (§3) |

Read top-to-bottom this is the deep-PC-traction result: PWL structure converts the **ill-posed iterative
inference** of smooth deep PC (§3) into a **single well-posed pass** (§4), so the PC–backprop gap stays
*bounded* — exactly zero for the pure-PWL net — where the smooth net's gap *grows*.

**Status (PRELIMINARY — to be finalized with the matched re-run).** This table states the intended contrast;
the numbers are being produced by a refined run. Three corrections over the first pass are required for the
result to be honest, all now in flight:

  1. **Matched, gradient-flowing backprop baselines.** Exact differentiation through the piecewise-constant
  dyadic quantization gives ≈ zero weight gradients (STE exists precisely to bypass this); the dyadic-BP arm
  must use the same STE the cascade does, and must be checked to actually train (loss descends).

  2. **A well-conditioned smooth net.** In the first pass the smooth relaxation *violated its own
  Whittington–Bogacz check*: run toward convergence it **diverged** at L=16 (bpb → uniform) rather than
  approaching backprop, because the residual highway makes ‖J‖ > 1 and the Jacobi settling amplifies rounding
  by ≈ ‖J‖^depth. That is a **solver-conditioning artifact, not honest truncation** — adding LayerNorm does not
  fix it (the net already has pre-LN; the residual identity path is the cause). The re-run bounds ‖J‖ (residual
  scaling) so that relaxation-to-convergence ≈ backprop is a *passing* gate; only then does the finite-T arm
  measure genuine depth truncation.

  3. **Convergence + train/val curves.** Longer training with logged train and held-out val trajectories per
  arm, to confirm each arm has plateaued before the gap is read.

§4's Remark and §5's measured mechanism stand on Experiment 1 alone (the clean, exact result). §6 is the
accuracy companion; its table is the target, its numbers pending the corrected run.
